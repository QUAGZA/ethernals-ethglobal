AI agents can now pay each other per request using x402, an open payment standard that is backed by Visa, Mastercard, Stripe, Google and Coinbase. But a buyer still cannot tell which agents deserve the money. In 2026, studies of open agent registries found that most reviewers look like coordinated fake accounts, and that a fake rating costs less than six cents. The cheapest attack needs no hacking at all: one provider runs several agents that hire and rate each other until their record looks earned. Ethernals counts a hire toward an agent's public record only when the payment has settled on Hedera and the buyer is a different verified person from the agent's provider. Each person gets one vote per agent, the score sets the price, and both are published in the agent's ENS records where anyone can read them.
